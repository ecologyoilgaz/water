Kazakhstan: Drinking Water Losses & Modular Pipe Plants

Primary objective: providing the population with safe drinking water through reducing network losses and transitioning to local production of composite pipes. All calculations are in US dollars.

1. Current State: Contradictory Data Requiring a Cautious Approach

Kazakhstan's open sources present two different datasets on water losses, and this in itself is part of the problem.

  • Bureau of National Statistics data (official statistics): water losses in networks in 2025 amounted to 6.8% (180.7 million m³ out of 2.66 billion m³ supplied to the network). By this indicator, Kazakhstan appears to be in good shape.
  • Industry and academic sources: network wear in certain regions reaches 60–70%, while water losses in the system average 20% nationwide. An academic study supported by the Ministry of Water Resources indicates that transmission losses in water supply systems average about 50% of supplied water, with total water network length at approximately 99,960 km, of which 18.6% is in worn condition.
Conservative estimate: The report adopts a 20% loss rate. The official 6.8% does not reflect the real picture, as it excludes hidden leaks in worn networks and commercial losses. The gap between 6.8% and 20% is the difference between reporting and physical reality.

2. Scale of the Problem in Monetary Terms

  • Network wear: up to 81.69% nationwide (Committee for Regulation of Natural Monopolies data), with 60–70% in certain regions.
  • Water losses: 20% (conservative). With 2.66 billion m³ supplied to the network, losses amount to ~532 million m³/year.
  • Cost of lost water: the exact nationwide tariff is not disclosed, but even at a conservative estimate of $0.10–0.15/m³, direct economic losses amount to $53–80 million/year. Including emergency repair costs, network relaying, and secondary water contamination — total damage exceeds $100 million/year.

5. Key Conclusions for Kazakhstan

Problem: Official statistics (6.8% losses) mask the real state of networks, whose wear reaches 60–70% in certain regions, while academic data indicates losses of up to 50%. The gap between reporting and reality is exactly the space where budget funds are lost and water does not reach consumers.

Solution: 20 modular plants by region. At a unified composite price ($149/m) and a confirmed difference from the cast iron analogue ($400/m), the annual economic effect amounts to ~$892 million, of which $502 million is direct savings on pipe material.

Caveat: Calculations use a unified regional composite pricing model ($149/m). Accurate validation for Kazakhstan requires commercial proposals from local manufacturers and data on actual water tariffs.
