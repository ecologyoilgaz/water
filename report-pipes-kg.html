Kyrgyzstan: Drinking Water Losses & Modular Pipe Plants

Primary objective: providing the population with safe drinking water through reducing network losses and transitioning to local production of composite pipes. All calculations are in US dollars.

1. Current State: Confirmed Data

For Kyrgyzstan, the data situation is more unambiguous than for Kazakhstan: official sources and international assessments converge on the conclusion that water losses are critical.

  • Minister of Water Resources data (2025): water supply infrastructure is worn by nearly 80%, water losses reach 55%. Solving the water supply problem for 960 villages requires $1.2 billion in investment.
  • National Statistical Committee data (2024): leakage and technological losses grew from 2,085.2 million m³ in 2018 to 2,417.3 million m³ in 2024. Nationwide, 25.9% to 27.8% of total withdrawn water does not reach consumers — every fourth cubic meter is lost.
  • Bishkek (SECO data): losses are estimated at 25% of water supplied to the network.
Working estimate: The report adopts 25–27% losses nationwide (per National Statistical Committee data), with peaks in certain regions of up to 55%.

2. Scale of the Problem in Monetary Terms

  • Water losses: 2,417.3 million m³ (2024).
  • Cost of lost water: at a conservative estimate of $0.10–0.15/m³, direct losses amount to $242–363 million/year. Including emergency repair costs and secondary contamination — total damage exceeds $400 million/year.
  • Access to water: in Bishkek, 15–20% of the population lacks access to piped drinking water. Nationwide, 960 villages lack reliable water supply.

3. Administrative Division and Number of Plants

Kyrgyzstan has 7 regions (Batken, Jalal-Abad, Issyk-Kul, Naryn, Osh, Talas, Chuy) and 2 cities of republican significance (Bishkek, Osh). Total: 9 administrative units.

Proposal: 9 compact modular plants, one per region.

5. Key Conclusions for Kyrgyzstan

Problem: Every fourth cubic meter of water (25.9–27.8%) is lost during transmission. Infrastructure is 80% worn, with losses reaching 55% in peak regions. Solving the problem requires $1.2 billion — and that is only for 960 villages, excluding trunk main modernization.

Solution: 9 modular plants by region. At a unified composite price ($149/m) and a confirmed difference from the cast iron analogue ($400/m), the annual economic effect amounts to ~$200 million, of which $113 million is direct savings on pipe material.

For a country requiring $1.2 billion in external financing, this means that local composite production can cover a significant portion of the investment need through savings on procurement and logistics.
Caveat: Calculations use a unified regional composite pricing model ($149/m). Accurate validation for Kyrgyzstan requires commercial proposals from local manufacturers and data on actual water tariffs.
